# SOP-305 — Heat exchanger cleaning

Document ID: `SOP-305`
Revision: `2.4`
Status: Approved

## [section:1.0] Scope

This procedure covers planned cleaning of shell-and-tube heat exchangers.
Cleaning may be chemical, mechanical, or a combination of both. The work
pack must identify the exchanger tag, fluid service, isolation boundary,
cleaning method, and waste-disposal route before work begins.

## [section:2.2] Pre-cleaning inspection

Review the latest inspection report and record the last known tube-wall
measurements, plugging history, gasket material, and shell-side fluid. Confirm
that the equipment is isolated and depressurised. A pre-cleaning inspection
must record visible fouling, corrosion, damaged nozzles, and any condition
that could make the proposed cleaning method unsuitable.

## [section:3.1] Mechanical cleaning controls

Use tools that match the tube material and diameter. Excessive brush force or
an incorrect lance can damage tube ends and expand existing pits. Record the
tool type, pass count, and any tube that cannot be cleaned. Do not treat a
cleaned surface as evidence that a tube is fit for service; fitness remains a
separate inspection decision.

## [section:4.3] Post-cleaning record

The completion record must list the exchanger tag, cleaning date, method,
waste transfer reference, and unresolved findings. If cleaning exposes a
through-wall leak, significant pitting, or gasket damage, stop the return to
service process and obtain an inspection disposition.
